The research methods behind it

None of this was invented for this site. It combines several established research methods, each with a name and published sources. Here they are in plain terms, and where each shows up.

A mixed-methods content analysis using Saldaña's two-cycle coding and codebook thematic analysis, with summative counts of scriptures, citations, and invitations tracked longitudinally across conferences.The approach, in one sentence, in the terms a researcher would use

Method (source)In plain termsWhere it shows up here
Qualitative content analysis
Mayring; Hsieh & Shannon		The umbrella name for the whole project: reading texts carefully and sorting what they say. Labels came from the talks themselves, and the counting came first, with interpretation after.The whole site
Two-cycle coding
Saldaña		First, short labels go on each small passage. Then those labels are condensed into bigger ideas.__CHAIN__themes
Codebook thematic analysis
Braun & Clarke; King (template analysis)		Finding themes while working from a written list of labels that carries over from one conference to the next, instead of starting from scratch each time.The master list of codes; the themes are rediscovered fresh each conference
Quantitative content analysis
Krippendorff; Neuendorf		Counting clearly defined things so anyone could check the numbers.Scripture references, leaders quoted, word counts, invitations (By the Numbers)
Quantitizing
Sandelowski and colleagues		Turning labels into numbers so ideas can be compared and charted.How many talks and sessions carried each theme
Longitudinal (trend) analysis
Saldaña		Following the same things over time to see what changes.October 2026 is the starting point; each new conference uses the same list of codes, so shifts can be compared
Corpus and text analytics
Baker		Purely computer-made word counts, with no human judgment.The word cloud and most-used words

These names describe how the reading and counting were done. They don't make the findings more than a description of this conference, and one reader did the coding, so another careful reader could group things differently.
