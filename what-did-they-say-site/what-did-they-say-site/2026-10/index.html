October 2026 General Conference

What did they say in the last general conference?

Why the name? President Dieter F. Uchtdorf once told of a Church member whose neighbor, on hearing that prophets and apostles speak to the world twice a year, asked exactly that. The member could not recall the details of a single talk, and resolved to remember better. This site is here to help all of us have an answer. Read the story in “General Conference—No Ordinary Blessing” (Liahona, Sept. 2011)

Here is one careful answer: every talk summarized, the patterns we noticed across all 37, every direct invitation in the speakers' own words, and a lesson to teach for each talk.

The 5-minute version

37 talks · 10 themes · 28 invitations
A pattern worth pondering

Witness and invitation was the pattern we noticed in the most talks: 25 of 37. Christ as the one who carries and lifts was close behind, in 23.

The arc

Saturday dwelt on trials (74% of the adversity material); Sunday turned to how we treat each other (86% of the truth-with-love material).

What the prophet invited

President Oaks extended four direct invitations: keep the commandments and be true to your covenants, believe the Book of Mormon's promises, act on these truths and share them, and study and ponder the conference talks.

The surprise

"Love" in its forms appears 263 times, far more than "covenant" (158) or "temple" (77).

President Dallin H. Oaks's invitations

The President of the Church extended these 4 direct invitations, in his own words. Add any of them to your plan.

“I urge you to keep the commandments and be true to the covenants so many of you have made to guide you along the covenant path.”
“These promises from the Book of Mormon are true. Millions believe them and have had experiences to affirm their truth. I invite all to believe them.”
“I call upon all to act on these truths and to share them with others that we may all draw closer to Jesus Christ, our Savior.”
“As the talks of this conference are published, we invite all to study and ponder them prayerfully and to act in faith on the principles they explain.”
