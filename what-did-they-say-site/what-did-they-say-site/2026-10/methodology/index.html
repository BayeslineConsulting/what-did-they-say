October 2026 General Conference

Full methodology

How a weekend of talks became the findings on this site, reported to the standard a peer-reviewed qualitative journal expects.

The analytic process

Census and transcriptionAll 37 addresses from the four general sessions, from machine transcripts of the broadcast; speakers identified from the conducting announcements.
SegmentationA manifest-driven script splits talks into 808 meaning units with stable IDs, so every claim can point to a unit.
First-cycle codingEvery unit read in full and given one to three codes (Initial, In Vivo, Process). A code exists only with a definition plus inclusion and exclusion rules.
Constant comparison and codebook refinementEach new unit is compared with units already in its code; definitions are tightened, and merges and rule changes are logged.
Second-cycle codingCodes are grouped into categories (axial), then integrated into ten themes stated as claims (selective). Rhetorical moves are coded but kept out of the themes.
Verification and theoryEvery quotation is checked by script against the transcript; theory is consulted only after the themes exist; a blind double-code tests agreement.

Design at a glance

Research questionsWhat did the conference teach, how was it distributed across speakers and sessions, and what did speakers ask listeners to do?
ApproachQualitative content analysis with thematic synthesis; descriptive claims only
CorpusCensus of all 37 addresses by 36 speakers in four general sessions (44,044 words)
Unit of analysisTranscript paragraph within an address (808 meaning units)
CodingTwo-cycle coding (Saldaña, 2013): 1,576 code applications, 124 codes, 26 categories, 10 themes
TheoryConsulted only after themes emerged
CoderSingle coder: an AI language model (Claude, Anthropic) following a written protocol
Audit trailSegmentation manifest, range-coded assignments, codebook and log, memos, scripts

Procedure in detail

DataMachine (speech-recognition) transcripts of the four broadcast sessions. Speakers identified from conducting announcements and talk content; eight garbled names are marked with an asterisk. Sustainings, conducting remarks, prayers, and music excluded.
First cycleAll 808 units read in full and coded in talk order, one to three codes each: Initial coding by default, In Vivo where the speaker's words carried the concept, Process coding for actions. A code was created only with a one-sentence definition and inclusion and exclusion rules. One analytic memo per talk.
Second cycleOpen, axial, and selective coding (Williams & Moser, 2019): codes grouped into 26 categories, then ten themes stated as claims. Constant comparison throughout. Rhetorical moves coded but held out of the themes. Theme count set by the principal investigator.
Action itemsPattern search for invitation language across all units, then a full reading of each talk's directive passages. Kept only items for members generally (subgroups flagged); promises excluded; each anchored to a verbatim quote.
MetricsWord counts, word frequencies, Christ-reference density, leader mentions, and recurring phrases produced by deterministic scripts. Scripture identified from the text by the coder.

Saturation

All 26 categories had appeared by the 10th talk; after that, talks added new codes (talk-specific concepts) at a rate of 0 to 3 each but no new categories. The thematic structure was saturated well before the corpus ended (Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006).

Trustworthiness

Lincoln and Guba's (1985) criteria for qualitative rigor, and the evidence for each in this study.

CredibilityAll 154 excerpts in the action list and example tables verified by script to appear word for word in the cited unit; transcription errors shown with bracketed corrections.
DependabilityRerunning segmentation reproduced all 808 units exactly; workbook counts are live formulas.
ConfirmabilityCodebook definitions, codebook log, and one memo per talk in the workbook.
TransferabilityClaims scoped to this conference; master codebook supports future comparison.
ReliabilityBlind double-code of one talk (17 units) prepared with Cohen's kappa; threshold 0.61 (Landis & Koch, 1977). Not yet completed.

Talk summaries and plan ideas

Each talk's summary was written for this site from a full reading of its transcript; any phrase in quotation marks was checked against the transcript. The ideas offered on the plan page are suggestions written for this site to help make each invitation specific. They are not the speakers' words, and the invitations themselves are quoted exactly as spoken.

Reflexivity and the AI coder

Coding was performed by an AI language model working from the written protocol; the principal investigator set the scope and is responsible for reviewing the output. An AI coder is consistent and fully documented but can repeat a blind spot across passes, so a human double-code is required before publication.

Limitations

  • Machine transcription garbles some names and words, so word counts are approximate.
  • Units follow transcript paragraph breaks, so some sentences are split.
  • Ten themes exceed the usual five to seven; themes 3 and 8 are broad but thin.
  • Theory mappings are cross-context and partly analogical (Venkatesh, Thong & Xu, 2016).
  • Single coder; agreement not yet measured.
  • Findings describe this conference only.

Reporting standards

Reporting follows the Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research (O'Brien et al., 2014) and the relevant COREQ items (Tong, Sainsbury & Craig, 2007). Thematic synthesis follows Braun and Clarke (2006); reliability follows O'Connor and Joffe's (2020) guidance for intercoder agreement in qualitative research.
