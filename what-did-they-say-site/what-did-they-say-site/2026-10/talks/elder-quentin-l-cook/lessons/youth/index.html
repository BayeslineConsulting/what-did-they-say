30-minute lesson · Youth · Ages 11–18: classes, quorums, or home

Same Person Everywhere

From “Integrity and Virtue Lead to Happiness,” by Elder Quentin L. Cook · Sunday morning session, October 2026 General Conference

AdultsYouthChildren
Watch the talkRead the full talk

The big idea

Living what we believe, with clean thoughts and words and with daily repentance, is how real happiness and the Holy Ghost come into our lives.

Objective

Youth will be able to say what integrity and virtue are in their own words, test their own words and media against Philippians 4:8, and choose one honest change to make this week.

Before you teach

  • Read Elder Cook’s talk, especially T24.04–T24.10, T24.15 and T24.22–T24.25.
  • Write each word of the inscription “WHAT-E’ER THOU ART ACT WELL THY PART” on its own slip of paper; make one mixed-up set for every three or four youth.
  • Bring paper and pens. Ask youth to have scriptures or the Gospel Library app ready.
  • Write the three scenarios from segment 3 on cards.

Lesson plan

Thirty minutes in 6 parts. Times are a guide; let the Spirit and the learners set the pace.

3 min0–3

Sixty-second happy list

  1. Start the moment they sit down. In teams of three or four, they have 60 seconds to write as many things as they can that people their age say will make them happy.
  2. Count the lists; cheer the longest. Then ask each team to circle the items that still make a person happy a year later.
  3. Say: Elder Quentin L. Cook of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles taught where lasting happiness comes from. Read the key quote from T24.10. Pray together.
Ask
  • Why do you think so few items got circled?
5 min3–8

Unscramble the stone

  1. Hand each team a set of word slips. First team to put the seven words in order wins. (They rhyme: art, part.)
  2. Tell the story: as a new missionary in Scotland, Elder David O. McKay, who later became President of the Church, passed a building with this message carved in stone above the doorway. Elder Cook said it influenced him as a missionary and for the rest of his life.
  3. Read the key quote from T24.05.
  4. Have each person write three parts they play right now (for example: friend, teammate, student, sister or brother, quorum or class member, disciple of Jesus Christ).
Ask
  • Put the inscription in your own words. How would you say it to a friend?
  • Which of your parts is easiest to do halfway?
7 min8–15

Integrity: three quick scenes

  1. Read President Kimball’s definition of integrity (aligned quote). Ask someone to restate it in ten words or fewer.
  2. On phones or in scriptures, have half the class read Doctrine and Covenants 124:20 and half read Luke 6:46. Each half tells the other what they found.
  3. Give each team one scenario card to act out in under a minute, showing what integrity would look like. These are made-up situations: (1) A group chat starts mocking a classmate and everyone is adding to it. (2) A friend offers you the answers to tonight’s assignment. (3) Someone is one person at church and a different person at practice or online.
  4. After each scene, ask the question below. Keep it honest; do not let anyone make the right choice look easy.
Ask
  • What makes this hard in real life, and what would make it possible?
  • Why would the Lord care whether He can trust us?
6 min15–21

The Philippians 4:8 filter

  1. Read the key quote from T24.15. Explain that Elder Cook also taught that the Lord has inspired marvelous technology that blesses us when used wisely. The phone is not the problem; how we use it can be.
  2. Read Philippians 4:8 together. As a class, pull out its tests and list them on the board.
  3. Private phone check, two minutes, no sharing: silently look at your last few messages, comments or posts. Would they pass the tests on the board? Nobody will ask what you found.
  4. Read Elder Kyungu’s words (aligned quote). Ask each team for one specific way to use a phone this week to lift someone.
Ask
  • Elder Cook said virtue includes cleanliness, modesty, goodness, holiness, and reverence for the sacred. Which of those is hardest to keep where you spend time, and what helps?
6 min21–27

What if I have already blown it?

  1. Say that this is a question many people carry: If I have done things I am not proud of, am I a fake for being here? Let them think before anyone answers.
  2. Read the key quote from T24.23. Point out that, according to Elder Cook, repenting is part of having integrity, not the opposite of it.
  3. Tell his story: as a young missionary in the British Mission he taught a man who had served on a battleship in World War II. When the man learned about repentance he was overcome. He said it felt like the day he came home to England, stepped off the ship, felt the earth of his island home under his feet, and fell to his knees in appreciation.
  4. Elder Cook reminded us that the Savior did not come to condemn the world but to save it. Invite one youth to read John 3:16–17.
  5. Share what Elder Cook called the ultimate reward of integrity and virtue: the constant companionship of the Holy Ghost. Bear a short testimony that the Savior makes coming home possible.
Ask
  • Why is it honest, not hypocritical, to keep coming to church while you are still repenting?
  • What does it feel like when the Holy Ghost is with you again?
3 min27–30

One honest change

  1. Check for understanding: each person writes one sentence finishing this thought: Integrity means… Have three volunteers read theirs.
  2. Show the ideas under Act this week. Each person picks one, or writes a better one, and puts the paper where they will see it.
  3. Share the promise Elder Cook gave: if we dedicate some time every day to the scriptures and spiritual renewal, we will be deeply blessed.
  4. Follow up next week: open by asking, Where were you the same person everywhere this week, and how did it feel?

Key passages from the talk

“In the Father’s plan, integrity, virtue, and happiness are intrinsically linked.”

Elder Cook’s talk, paragraph 10

“He determined that whatever responsibility he had, he would do his very best.”

Elder Cook’s talk, paragraph 5

“Any use of the internet to bully, destroy a reputation, or place a person in a bad light is reprehensible.”

Elder Cook’s talk, paragraph 15

“Part of having integrity is acknowledging our faults to the Lord and repenting of transgressions.”

Elder Cook’s talk, paragraph 23

Scriptures

  • Doctrine and Covenants 124:20 Half the class reads it and tells the other half why the Lord said this servant could be trusted.
  • Luke 6:46 The other half reads it and explains the gap the Savior is asking about.
  • Philippians 4:8 Read together; list its tests on the board and use them as a filter for messages, posts and media.
  • John 3:16–17 One youth reads both verses aloud after the battleship story; ask what verse 17 says about why the Savior came.

Other voices that agree

“integrity (the willingness and ability to live by our beliefs and commitments) is one of the foundation stones of good character”

President Spencer W. Kimball, quoted in this talk (paragraph 9)

“Let us use the language that uplifts, edifies, comforts, and brings people together.”

Elder Kyungu · Using the Language of Peacemakers, paragraph 16

Three things to remember

  • Integrity is being the same disciple of Jesus Christ everywhere.
  • What I think, post and say shapes how happy I am.
  • Repenting every day is part of integrity, and it brings the Holy Ghost back.

Ways to act this week

  • Pick one of your parts and do it all the way for seven days.
  • Run every post, comment and message this week through Philippians 4:8 before you send it.
  • Send one message a day that lifts somebody.
  • Spend ten minutes a day in the scriptures, then tell Heavenly Father honestly about one thing you want to change.

Adapting the lesson

  • At home or with two or three youth: do the unscramble as a race against a timer, and talk through the scenarios instead of acting them out.
  • No phones available: skip the private phone check and have each person silently recall the last three things they said about someone who was not present.
  • Short on time: keep segments 2, 5 and 6.
