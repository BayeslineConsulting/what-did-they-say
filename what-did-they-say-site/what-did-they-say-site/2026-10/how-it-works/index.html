How it works

How we read the talks

A simple explanation of qualitative coding, the careful, line-by-line reading researchers use to find patterns, and how those patterns connect to what we were asked to do.

Read every lineAll 37 talks were divided into 808 short passages, each holding a single idea, and every one was read.
Tag what's saidEach passage got up to three short labels, called codes, naming what the speaker was doing: teaching a doctrine, telling a story, making a promise, extending an invitation. 124 codes in all.
Group the tagsCodes that belong together were grouped into categories, and categories into ten broader themes. The themes came from the talks, not from a list decided in advance.
Count with careEvery theme is backed by passages you can read. We count how many talks carried it, and in which sessions, so patterns rest on evidence rather than impressions.
Find every invitationSeparately, every sentence where a speaker directly asked us to do something was collected, checked word for word, and linked to the theme it fits.
Turn it into a planThose invitations become your six-month plan: choose a few, make them specific, and check in each week until the next conference.

What this can show you

  • Which ideas came up again and again, across many speakers.
  • How the weekend moved, such as trials on Saturday and treating each other well on Sunday.
  • Every place a speaker directly asked us to act, in their exact words.
  • Related passages from different talks, side by side.

What it can't

  • Tell you what the Lord wants you to focus on. That comes through prayer and the Spirit.
  • Speak for the speakers or for the Church. This is an independent study.
  • Replace the talks. Read, watch, and listen to them in full.
  • Give the only right grouping. Another careful reader might organize things differently; the themes are lenses for pondering, not the final word.

What counts as “asked to do”

Only explicit requests count: sentences where a speaker directly invites, urges, asks, or pleads (“I invite you…,” “let us…,” “please…”) or gives a direct instruction (“Keep your covenants,” “Never give up”). Statements of truth, stories, “may we” wishes, and descriptions are not counted, however inspiring. This conference yielded 34 invitations from 27 talks, drawn from 76 exact quotations, beginning with President Oaks's 9. Invitations that several speakers extended are grouped, and every speaker who asked is named.
